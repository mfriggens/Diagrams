Part A | Evolution of the IPCC Vulnerability Concept
TAR / AR4  (IPCC 2001, 2007)
Vulnerability-Centered
V = f(Exposure, Sensitivity, AC)
Exposure is a hazard-centered concept included within vulnerability. All three components are integrated into a single vulnerability score.
Revised
SREX / AR5 / AR6 (IPCC 2012-2022)
Risk-Centered
Risk = f(Hazard, Exposure, Vulnerability)
V = f(Sensitivity, Capacity to cope & adapt)
Exposure is separated from vulnerability and redefined as elements at risk. Vulnerability narrows to sensitivity + adaptive capacity.
Parallel
Refugia Conservation Cycle  (Morelli et al. 2020, 2026)
Refugia-Centered
Refugia Potential = f(CC Exposure, Conservation Capacity)
Cons. Capacity = Sensitivity + AC (ecological & management)
Climate change exposure is the external climate context. Conservation capacity integrates sensitivity and adaptive capacity of the system.

Ability to adjust, cope with, or exploit climate change. Moderates vulnerability. May be intrisic or extrinsic. May be weighted against Sensitivity or Impact.

Core component of revised vulnerability; shares equal structural weight with sensitivity.

Part C | RRA Strategy Alignment
Strategy 1
Resistance
Minimize disturbance; maintain current ecological structure and function. Appropriate where climate exposure is low relative to conservation capacity or high-refugia potential.
Low CC Exposure High Cons. Capacity Low Sensitivity Strong AC
Strategy 2
Resilience
Enhance ability to absorb stress and recover. Intermediate exposure and capacity; refugia potential present but conditional. Management can augment conservation capacity.
Moderate CC Exposure Moderate Sensitivity Moderate AC Conditional Refugia
Strategy 3
Transition
Facilitate ecological transformation toward novel states. High exposure overwhelms conservation capacity; current conditions unsustainable. Low or no refugia potential.
High CC Exposure High Sensitivity Limited AC Low Refugia Potential
Figure X. Convergence of IPCC vulnerability frameworks and the Refugia Conservation Cycle (RCC), and their joint alignment with Resistance-Resilience-Transition (RRA) management strategies. Part A traces the evolution of the IPCC vulnerability concept from the TAR/AR4 (vulnerability = exposure + sensitivity + adaptive capacity) through the SREX/AR5/AR6 revision, in which exposure was separated from vulnerability and redefined as elements at risk, while vulnerability was reconceptualized as a function of sensitivity and capacity to cope and adapt. This revised structure is structurally equivalent to the RCC, in which climate change exposure is an external driver and conservation capacity integrates sensitivity and adaptive capacity. Part B maps component-level correspondences across all three frameworks, demonstrating that despite different terminologies and disciplinary origins, all three share the same underlying logical architecture: an external climate driver acting upon a target whose intrinsic response is a function of sensitivity and adaptive capacity. Part C shows how the integrated output of each framework (vulnerability score, risk level, or refugia potential) maps onto RRA strategy space, with high-refugia / low-vulnerability sites favoring Resistance strategies and high-exposure / low-capacity sites necessitating Transition. Colors follow the Wong (2011) colorblind-safe palette.
